## Q1. 왜 럭비공은 타원형(장구형)인가 — 역사와 World Rugby Law 2 규격

### Takeaway
The shape began with the pig's bladder inside a leather case (plum-shaped, not standardized); India-rubber bladders (Richard Lindon, c.1862) made shape controllable and balls became more egg-shaped as the game moved to carrying/passing. Official dimensions were first written into the Laws in 1892. Current Law 2: oval, 4 panels, 410–460 g, 65.71–68.75 kPa; dimension figures appear in the law diagram (confirmed in the 2015 Laws PDF).

### Cited Findings
- Bootmaker William Gilbert made hand-stitched leather cases around inflated pig's bladders for Rugby School pupils; bladder shape "thought to be" the origin of the form; early balls were plum-shaped, size depended on the bladder (smaller balls for younger pupils). **[확인]** — [World Rugby Museum: The evolution of the rugby ball](https://www.worldrugbymuseum.com/from-the-vaults/evolution-of-rugby/the-evolution-of-the-rugby-ball)
- Richard Lindon sought a safer alternative after his wife died of lung disease in 1843, "possibly" from inflating bladders by mouth (causal link is uncertain). Inspired by the 1851 Great Exhibition, he pioneered the India-rubber bladder inflated by a brass hand pump; this made size and shape controllable and balls "more egg-shaped" as the game shifted to carrying and passing. Lindon did not patent it. **[확인]** — same World Rugby Museum page
- By 1877 Gilbert's workshop made about 2,800 balls per year. **[확인]** — same page
- RFU formed 1871; ball dimensions first regulated in the Laws in 1892 (length, circumferences, weight 12–13 oz, amended next season to 13–14½ oz); four-panel ball required from 1960–61; leather no longer required 1969/70; Mitre synthetic ball 1970; Gilbert's last leather international ball 1986. **[확인]** — same page
- Dating conflict: Wikipedia says Lindon introduced rubber inner tubes in 1870 and the shape "gradually changed from a sphere to an egg"; the Museum ties rubber bladders to ~1862. Treat 1862 as start of rubber bladders, 1870 as a commonly cited date. **[스니펫]** — [Wikipedia: Rugby ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_ball)
- Current World Rugby Law 2 text (online): "The ball is oval and made of four panels"; leather or suitable synthetic; "may be treated to make it water resistant and easier to grip"; weighs 410–460 g; air pressure at start of play 65.71–68.75 kPa (0.67–0.70 kg/cm², 9.5–10.0 psi); match organisers may permit smaller balls. Dimensions are given only in a diagram (2.2). **[확인]** — [World Rugby Laws, Law 2 Ball](https://passport.world.rugby/laws-of-the-game/laws-by-number/2-ball/)
- Dimensions (text extracted from World Rugby "Laws of the Game Rugby Union 2015", Law 2.2): Length in line 280–300 mm; Circumference (end to end) 740–770 mm; Circumference (in width) 580–620 mm. Also 2.4 weight 410–460 g; 2.5 pressure 65.71–68.75 kPa. **[확인]** — [World Rugby PDF (2015 Laws, Law 2)](https://passport.world.rugby/media/i0ejgzmf/ball_pressure_en.pdf)
- World Rugby Museum page also states (as of 2024) 28–30 cm long, 58–62 cm widest circumference, 410–460 g — consistent with above. **[확인]** — [World Rugby Museum](https://www.worldrugbymuseum.com/from-the-vaults/evolution-of-rugby/the-evolution-of-the-rugby-ball)

### Inferences
- Classroom-friendly numbers: 길이 약 28~30 cm, 둘레(긴 방향) 74~77 cm, 둘레(짧은 방향) 58~62 cm, 무게 약 410~460 g (스마트폰 2~3개 정도), 공기압 약 66~69 kPa (대기압 약 101 kPa에 "더해진" 게이지 압력으로 보는 것이 일반적 — this gauge-pressure interpretation is my inference, not stated in the law text).
- "왜 타원형?" for students: (1) 역사적으로 돼지 방광 모양에서 출발, (2) 고무 튜브 이후 모양을 조절할 수 있게 되었고, 들고 뛰고 패스하는 경기 방식에 맞춰 달걀형이 됨. Avoid stating "it was designed for passing" as sole cause — sources say shape evolved alongside the carrying game.

### Gaps
- Whether the current (2024–26) online law diagram numbers are identical to the 2015 PDF: online text confirms weight/pressure only; diagram image not read. Very likely unchanged (Museum's 2024 figures match).
- The "1870s–1890s standardization" range: only the 1892 RFU law date is confirmed.

## Q2. 왜 럭비공은 어디로 튈지 모를까 (불규칙한 바운드)

### Takeaway
The bounce follows physics but depends on how the ball is tilted at contact: because the ball is elongated, the ground's push often does not pass through the centre of mass, so the ball gets spun and kicked off in different directions. Small differences in landing angle → big differences in bounce.

### Cited Findings
- University of Auckland physicist Geoff Willmott (2015): a bounce looks irregular but its direction can be reasoned from the angle at which the ball hits; the ball is ellipsoidal, and even landing vertically the reaction force "may not act through its centre of mass"; the collision is inelastic (coefficient of restitution = energy loss); simpler if the ball didn't deform, but it does. **[확인]** — [RWC Physics Blog V: Final Thoughts](https://condensed-matter.blogs.auckland.ac.nz/?p=382)
- Peer-reviewed: R. Cross, "Bounce of an oval shaped football," Sports Technology 3(3):168–180 (2010) (listed on author's publication page **[확인 – listing only]**). Summary claim that the inclination angle strongly biases all bounce parameters and that the "erratic" bounce can be described by simple physics laws **[스니펫]** — [Rod Cross, Physics of Sport publications](https://www.physics.usyd.edu.au/~cross/PHYSICS%20OF%20SPORT.htm)
- Research on rugby players: oval balls "display specific bounce characteristics based on the nature of ball-flight", and skill level affects anticipation of bounce. **[스니펫]** — [Univ. of Chichester eprint: anticipation of ball-bounce in rugby union](https://eprints.chi.ac.uk/id/eprint/4932/)
- Waikato physics blog: a rugby ball easily tumbles in flight, unlike a soccer ball with equal axes. **[스니펫]** — [Univ. of Waikato Physics Stop blog](https://blog.waikato.ac.nz/physicsstop/?p=453)
- A student modeling paper found contact angles near 45° change horizontal velocity most; near horizontal/vertical least (American football, student-level work, low authority). **[스니펫]** — [UNC Charlotte, Reading the Bounce](https://cstem.charlotte.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/267/2023/05/Reading-the-Bounce-Modeling-a-Footballs-Bounce-Rishi-Goswami.pdf)

### Inferences
- Student-level explanation: 축구공은 어느 점으로 떨어져도 바닥이 미는 힘이 공의 중심을 향함 → 거의 예측 가능하게 튐. 럭비공은 뾰족한 끝·옆면 등 어디가 닿느냐에 따라 바닥이 미는 힘(떠받치는 힘)이 중심을 벗어나 → 공이 돌면서 엉뚱한 방향으로 튐.
- **[교육과정 내]**: 힘의 방향(화살표), 탄성력, 바닥이 떠받치는 힘, 중력. **[교육과정 밖 → 정성적]**: 무게중심·토크·회전, 반발계수.
- Activity idea (inference): 같은 높이에서 럭비공을 서 있게/눕혀서/비스듬히 떨어뜨려 튀는 방향 비교.

### Gaps
- Did not read Cross (2010) full text; no specific numbers on bounce angles for a rugby ball.

## Q3. 스핀(나선) 패스는 왜 더 멀리, 더 정확히 갈까

### Takeaway
Spinning the ball about its long axis makes it gyroscopically stable, so it keeps its nose pointing forward instead of tumbling; a nose-first ball presents a smaller face to the air, so air resistance is smaller → flies farther and straighter, and is easier to catch.

### Cited Findings
- Willmott (Univ. of Auckland, 2015): ball spinning about its long axis while travelling along that axis "travels further because the air resistance on the ball is relatively small"; less surface facing the motion → weaker drag; gyroscopic effect means "a spinning rugby ball is prevented from tumbling end-over-end"; notes the spiral pass has similar physics to the spiral punt. Swerve is mainly from the Magnus effect. **[확인]** — [RWC Physics Blog III: Spirals and Swerves](https://condensed-matter.blogs.auckland.ac.nz/2015/10/08/rwc-physics-blog-iii-spirals-and-swerves/)
- Seo et al. (2003 ISB abstract; 2006 Sports Engineering, screw kick): wind-tunnel measurements on spinning and non-spinning full-size rugby balls at 20 m/s; drag, lift, pitching moment depend on angle of attack; side force (Magnus) depends on spin rate and angle of attack; players believe spin extends distance and a spinning pass is easier to catch due to gyroscopic stability. **[스니펫]** — [ISB 2003 short abstract (Seo et al.)](https://media.isbweb.org/images/conf/2003/shortAbstracts/SEO_396-432_SPO_E.pdf); [Flight dynamics of the screw kick in rugby](https://www.bisp-surf.de/Record/PU201103001727)
- Price, Moss & Gay (2020, American Journal of Physics): American-football spiral — without air the ball is a torque-free gyroscope; a well-thrown spiral keeps its long axis roughly aligned with its path due to aerodynamic torque + gyroscopic action (same geometry as rugby). **[스니펫]** — [Price et al. 2020 PDF (UNL)](https://physics.unl.edu/~tgay/publications/papers/Price%20et%20al.%20-%202020%20-%20The%20paradox%20of%20the%20tight%20spiral%20pass%20in%20American%20f.pdf)
- Alam et al. (2012): drag coefficients of American football, rugby and Australian Rules balls are close to each other. **[스니펫]** — [Aston research: aerodynamics of oval-shaped sports balls](https://research.aston.ac.uk/en/publications/aerodynamics-of-oval-shaped-sports-balls/)

### Inferences
- 비유: 팽이·자전거 바퀴가 돌 때 쓰러지지 않는 것처럼 회전하는 공은 자세를 유지함. 뾰족한 끝으로 공기를 가르면 공기가 미는 힘(공기 저항=일종의 마찰)이 작아짐.
- **[교육과정 내]**: 공기 저항을 "운동을 방해하는 힘(마찰)"으로 설명 가능; 알짜힘과 운동 변화. **[교육과정 밖 → 정성적]**: 자이로(각운동량) 안정, 마그누스 효과, 항력계수.
- Note for tag-rugby lesson: Most school/tag rugby passes are short "pop/lateral" passes; spin pass is a skill extension.

## Q4. 전진 패스(스로 포워드) 착시 — 상대 운동

### Takeaway
World Rugby's definition judges a throw forward by the passer's arms ("if the arms of the player passing the ball move forward"), while "forward" means towards the opposition's dead-ball line. Because the passer is running, a ball thrown backward relative to the passer can still travel forward over the ground — this is relative motion (inertia), not cheating.

### Cited Findings
- Law 11.7: "A player must not intentionally throw or pass the ball forward." (Penalty); 11.6 throw forward may occur anywhere (scrum). No mention of arms/hands in Law 11 itself. **[확인]** — [World Rugby Laws, Law 11](https://passport.world.rugby/laws-of-the-game/laws-by-number/11-knock-forward-or-throw-forward/)
- Definitions: **Throw forward** = "When a player throws or passes the ball forward i.e. if the arms of the player passing the ball move forward." **Forward** = "Towards the opposition's dead-ball line." **Pass** = "A player throws or hands the ball to another player." **[확인]** — [World Rugby Laws, Definitions](https://passport.world.rugby/laws-of-the-game/definitions/)
- Physics explainer (Willmott, Univ. of Auckland, 1 Oct 2015): passer and spectator are in different frames; subtract the passer's velocity from the ball's velocity → the ball can travel forward relative to the field while travelling backward relative to the passer; the accepted interpretation (shown in a referee video) treats a pass as legal when the ball moves backward in the passer's frame; advice: watch the ball as it leaves the hands, field lines irrelevant; "momentum" is a misused word here — only velocity matters; he notes the law's "forward" definition does not specify a frame of reference (ambiguity). Analogy: bouncing a tennis ball on a moving train. **[확인]** — [RWC Physics Blog II: Forward Passing the Buck](https://condensed-matter.blogs.auckland.ac.nz/?p=344)
- John Barrow (Plus Magazine, 2003) framed the forward pass as a relativity-of-motion problem. **[스니펫]** — [Plus Magazine](https://plus.maths.org/content/comment/5416)
- Commentary: 2023 Six Nations, Duhan van der Merwe try vs England — many fans thought the pass forward, but by the law it was backward from the hands. **[스니펫]** — [RugbyPass](https://rugbypass.com/plus/how-the-forward-pass-has-rugby-tied-up-in-knots); columnist note that a stationary camera/spectator sees a pass between running players appear forward, a running referee sees differently **[스니펫]** — [The Rugby Paper, Nick Cain](https://www.therugbypaper.co.uk/all/columnists/nick-cain/473350/referees-must-be-told-to-cut-out-the-forward-pass/)
- No formal empirical study of referee perception of forward passes was found in my searches.

### Inferences (illustrative numbers — my arithmetic, not from a source)
- 앞쪽을 +, 뒤쪽을 − 로 정함. 달리는 선수 +6 m/s, 선수가 손으로 공을 뒤로 4 m/s 던짐(선수 기준 −4 m/s) → 땅(관중) 기준 공의 빠르기 = 6 + (−4) = **+2 m/s (앞으로 이동!)**. 그래도 선수의 팔/손은 뒤로 던졌으므로 규칙상 합법 패스.
- 반대로 선수가 멈춰 있으면(0 m/s) 같은 패스는 −4 m/s로 확실히 뒤로 감.
- 연결 개념: 관성 — 공은 손을 떠나기 전 선수와 함께 +6 m/s로 달리고 있었으므로 그 운동을 "가지고" 나감 (정성적).
- Simplification for students: "규칙은 땅의 선이 아니라 선수의 팔(손) 방향을 본다." Mention that the definition text uses "arms"; many commentators say "hands".
- **[교육과정 내]**: 직선 위 속력, 등속 운동, 관성(정성적; 알짜힘 0이면 운동 유지는 [9과05-03]과 연계). **[교육과정 밖 → 정성적/간단 산수만]**: 기준틀, 상대 속도, 벡터 뺄셈 (중학교는 "나란한 힘의 합력"까지만).
- Also notable: forward pass called "knock forward/throw forward" in current laws; "knock-on" renamed "knock forward" (definitions say "previously knock-on"). **[확인]** — [Definitions](https://passport.world.rugby/laws-of-the-game/definitions/)

## Q5. 그 밖의 재미있는 과학 (짧게)

### Takeaway
Pressure is legally specified (gas pressure, temperature effects link to 기체의 성질); ball surface can be treated for grip (friction); tag belts use hook-and-loop (Velcro), a biomimicry invention from burrs.

### Cited Findings
- Law 2: pressure specified "at the start of play" 65.71–68.75 kPa; ball may be treated "to make it water resistant and easier to grip." **[확인]** — [World Rugby Law 2](https://passport.world.rugby/laws-of-the-game/laws-by-number/2-ball/)
- Gilbert smart ball with a chip tracking kick distance and spin rate first used in international rugby at the 2021 Women's Six Nations. **[확인]** — [World Rugby Museum](https://www.worldrugbymuseum.com/from-the-vaults/evolution-of-rugby/the-evolution-of-the-rugby-ball)
- Velcro: George de Mestral, after a 1941 Alps walk, saw under a microscope that burdock burrs have tiny hooks that catch loops in fabric/fur; developed with weavers in Lyon, nylon proved better than cotton; ~10 years to mechanize; Swiss patent 1954 or 1955 (sources differ), US patent 1955; name from French velours (velvet) + crochet (hook). **[스니펫]** — [National Inventors Hall of Fame](https://www.invent.org/blog/inventors/walk-in-the-woods-velcro); [hookandloop.com patent history](https://www.hookandloop.com/blog/history-velcro-patent)

### Inferences
- 공기압-온도: 추운 날 공 안 기체 입자 운동이 느려져 압력이 낮아짐 → 공이 덜 탱탱 → 덜 튐 ([9과06-01], [9과06-03] 기체 입자 운동 모델로 설명 가능, **교육과정 내**). No rugby-specific source found; label as 일반 원리.
- 공 표면 돌기("pimples"/grip pattern): 표면이 거칠수록 손과 공 사이 마찰력 ↑ → 미끄러지지 않음 (**마찰력, 교육과정 내**). Source only for "treated ... easier to grip"; dimple pattern details not sourced.
- 무릎 굽혀 방향 전환: 무게중심을 낮추고 발이 땅을 비스듬히 밀어 마찰력으로 방향을 바꿈 → 알짜힘이 운동 방향을 바꾼다 ([9과05-03], **교육과정 내**; 무게중심 개념은 정성적). No source found — teacher inference.
- 태그 벨트: 갈고리(hook)와 고리(loop)가 걸리는 힘 > 살짝 당기는 힘이면 붙어 있고, 세게 당기면 떨어짐 (힘의 크기 비교, 정성적).

## Q6. 재미있는 기원 이야기 — 웨브 엘리스 전설, 웨브 엘리스 컵, 태그럭비

### Takeaway
The 1823 William Webb Ellis "picked up the ball and ran" story has no first-hand evidence and is widely treated as a myth (even the World Rugby Museum calls him a "King Arthur" figure), but the Rugby World Cup trophy is still the Webb Ellis Cup. Tag rugby: a Gibraltar-origin variant, codified for children in England by PE teacher Nick Leonard (1990; first schools festival 1991) using Velcro belts and ribbons.

### Cited Findings
- Webb Ellis legend: first put forward by Matthew Bloxam (letters 1876, saying 1824; 1880, saying 1823), not a contemporary and quoting an unnamed source; 1895 Old Rugbeian Society sub-committee "unable to procure any first hand evidence"; Thomas Hughes recalled the legend "had not survived to my day". **[스니펫]** — [Wikipedia: William Webb Ellis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Webb_Ellis)
- Webb Ellis: born 24 Nov 1806 (Salford), died 24 Feb 1872 (Menton, France); plaque at Rugby School "erected in 1900" per Wikipedia, inscription ending "A.D. 1823". **[확인 – Wikipedia]** — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Webb_Ellis). Conflict: the brief says "plaque 1895"; the 1895 date is the Old Rugbeian inquiry, while Wikipedia gives 1900 for the plaque. Use "1895 조사 / 1900 기념비" or avoid the plaque year.
- World Rugby Museum 2023 special exhibition "Enigma: The William Webb Ellis Story"; curator Michael Rowe: very little or no evidence; Webb Ellis "little more than a 'King Arthur' figure". **[스니펫]** — [World Rugby Museum exhibition news](https://worldrugbymuseum.com/news/exhibitions/new-special-exhibition-at-the-world-rugby-museum-enigma-the-william-webb-ellis-story); [Twickenham Stadium news](https://www.twickenhamstadium.com/news/world-rugby-museum/world-rugby-museum-introduce-new-special-exhibition)
- Webb Ellis Cup = trophy presented to Rugby World Cup winners. **[확인 – Wikipedia]** — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Webb_Ellis)
- Tag rugby: first developed in Gibraltar by the Gibraltar RFU due to lack of grass pitches (a ~25 cm cord tucked in waistband, removed with shout "tag"); PE teacher Nick Leonard codified a children's version in England in 1990 (idea from ex-serviceman Barry Johns), with belts and coloured ribbons attached by Velcro; first schools tag rugby festival at UCP Marjon, Plymouth, 1991. Separate Australian lineage (Oztag, Perry Haddock, 1992) from a training aid for rugby league. **[스니펫]** — [Wikipedia: Tag rugby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tag_rugby); [Wikipedia: Oztag](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oztag)
